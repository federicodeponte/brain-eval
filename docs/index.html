Can a model read a brain?

Frontier models get raw spike counts from real neurons and three labeled examples, with no training, and must say what the brain was seeing. We score them against a classic decoder that gets the same examples, and against you. Then we hand an agent broken data and check whether it notices.

The hard part

Give an agent broken data: Codex claims 83% and gets 42%

A coding agent gets real recordings, builds a decoder and reports how accurate it is. Some copies of the data carry one hidden fault. Plain code scores the real accuracy on a hidden test, the gap to the claim, and whether the fault was named. Bars: Codex (gpt-5.3-codex), 3 passes over 24 tasks. Claude Opus on the same tasks: claimed 72%, got 63% on broken data, named 11 of 12 faults. The benchmark tells agents apart.

Your turn

Forty neurons in the amygdala and hippocampus of epilepsy patients, recorded through clinical depth electrodes while they looked at photos. Each row is how many spikes every neuron fired in the second after a photo appeared. Three labeled rows per category, then a new row. What was the person looking at?

Results

Opus reads neurons as well as a classic decoder

Every question, same examples for every solver, one shot, no tools. Human 40 questions, mouse 60 and 30, hard tier 20 each. The decoder is nearest-centroid on the labeled rows in the prompt. Opus ties it on all three tasks, yet gets different questions right: on human neurons, 5 that only Opus solved and 4 that only the decoder solved. On the hard tier, with one example per class, Opus gets 50% (human) and 60% (mouse, 8 clips): still level with the decoder and far from solved. Haiku bars are from 10 questions.

Chance Haiku Opus Decoder

At n=40 a bar is about ±15 points, at n=60 about ±9. When Opus may run Python, it writes a nearest-centroid decoder and makes the same picks as the decoder.

Is it really learning?

Six checks

Swapped labels

Car photos are labeled "food" in the examples. A model that reads the neurons follows the labels; one that relies on brain trivia picks the real category. Opus followed the examples 12 of 20 times and the real category 3 of 20.

Passed

Learning curve

With 0 examples the model must sit at chance; accuracy should climb with examples. With 0 examples Opus answered "A" every time. With 1 example it got 7 of 10.

Passed

No brain words

The same 20 questions, relabeled as pulse counts from 40 machine sensors in modes A-D. No neurons, photos or brain regions. Opus got 13 of 20 both ways. It reads the number patterns in the examples, not neuroscience knowledge.

Same score

Swapped clip

The right answer's examples are replaced with a clip from another movie. The correct reply is "none of these".

Built

Shuffled spikes

Real firing rates with the structure destroyed. Anything above chance means a leak or a letter bias.

Built

Planted pattern

An obvious pattern planted in synthetic data. Should be near 100%, which checks the harness and the answer parser.
